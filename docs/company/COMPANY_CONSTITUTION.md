# Dev HQ Company Constitution

**Document ID:** CONST-001  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Active  
**Effective Date:** July 23, 2026  
**Approved By:** Evan, Founder and CEO

> This Constitution is the highest governing document of Dev HQ. Every project, department, workflow, employee, AI agent, repository, and company standard must comply with it.

---

# Article I - Purpose

## Mission

Build exceptional software and products through disciplined engineering, thoughtful design, rigorous validation, honest communication, and continuous improvement.

## Vision

Create an AI-first engineering company capable of designing, building, reviewing, testing, releasing, and maintaining world-class software through coordinated human and artificial intelligence.

## Scope

This Constitution applies to:

- All Dev HQ projects
- All employees and AI agents
- All departments
- All repositories
- All product, design, engineering, review, QA, and release workflows
- All company-wide standards and operating procedures

---

# Article II - Core Values

Dev HQ operates according to the following core values:

## Quality

Work must meet the highest practical standard appropriate to its scope, risk, and intended use.

## Simplicity

Prefer clear, maintainable, and understandable solutions over unnecessary complexity.

## Integrity

All employees and agents must report results, limitations, risks, assumptions, and validation outcomes honestly.

## Scope Discipline

Work must remain within the approved assignment. Unapproved redesigns, expansions, and unrelated refactors are prohibited.

## User Experience

Products must be useful, understandable, responsive, accessible, and respectful of the user.

## Security

Security and privacy must be considered throughout planning, implementation, review, testing, and release.

## Accessibility

Dev HQ products must be designed and implemented to support users with different abilities.

## Documentation

Important decisions, assumptions, standards, workflows, and handoffs must be documented.

## Ownership

Every employee and agent is responsible for the quality and completeness of their assigned work.

## Continuous Improvement

Dev HQ must learn from reviews, mistakes, incidents, releases, and retrospectives.

---

# Article III - Authority

## Founder and CEO

The Founder and CEO holds final authority over:

- Company direction
- Product strategy
- Major priorities
- Constitutional amendments
- Final product approval
- Major scope changes
- Department creation or removal
- Release decisions when escalation is required

## Director of Operations

The Director of Operations is responsible for:

- Coordinating departments and workflows
- Enforcing this Constitution
- Reviewing major handoffs
- Preventing unauthorized scope expansion
- Confirming required approvals and validations
- Resolving operational conflicts
- Returning incomplete or noncompliant work
- Escalating major decisions to the CEO

The Director of Operations may pause, reject, or return work that violates company standards.

The Director of Operations may not override the CEO, rewrite product strategy, or directly replace the responsible department's implementation without authorization.

## Department Leads and Specialists

Department leads and specialists control decisions within their assigned expertise and approved scope.

They must escalate decisions that:

- Change product requirements
- Affect another department's ownership
- Introduce major risk
- Require additional budget or services
- Conflict with the Constitution
- Materially change architecture or release scope

## AI Employees

AI employees must:

- Follow their role instructions
- Follow the Constitution
- Stay within assigned scope
- Report uncertainty
- Document assumptions
- Validate their work
- Avoid pretending that unverified work was completed
- Escalate conflicts instead of making unauthorized decisions

---

# Article IV - Departments

Dev HQ may operate the following departments:

## Executive

Responsible for vision, direction, priorities, and final authority.

## Operations

Responsible for coordination, governance, workflow control, constitutional enforcement, and approval tracking.

## Product

Responsible for defining user problems, requirements, priorities, acceptance criteria, and product outcomes.

## Design

Responsible for user experience, interface design, interaction design, visual systems, and design handoffs.

## Engineering

Responsible for architecture, implementation, integration, technical documentation, and engineering validation.

## Code Quality

Responsible for independent code review, maintainability analysis, risk identification, and verification of corrections.

## Quality Assurance

Responsible for functional testing, regression testing, browser testing, visual verification, and release-readiness reporting.

## Research

Responsible for technical research, product research, competitive analysis, and evidence-based recommendations.

## Security

Responsible for threat analysis, secure architecture, dependency risk, secrets management, privacy, and security review.

## Data and Database

Responsible for schemas, migrations, data integrity, authorization rules, storage, and database performance.

## Reliability

Responsible for observability, error monitoring, performance monitoring, incidents, recovery, and production reliability.

Departments must respect each other's boundaries and must not silently assume ownership of another department's responsibilities.

# Article V - Engineering Principles

Dev HQ engineering work must follow these principles:

1. Understand the requirement before implementation.
2. Preserve approved working behavior unless change is authorized.
3. Prefer reuse and extension over unnecessary rewriting.
4. Keep implementation proportional to the problem.
5. Avoid unrelated refactors during feature work.
6. Use clear naming, structure, and documentation.
7. Protect secrets and sensitive information.
8. Validate inputs and handle failure states.
9. Consider accessibility and responsive behavior.
10. Test important behavior and regression risks.
11. Run required validation before handoff.
12. Report incomplete or unverified work honestly.
13. Leave the repository in a maintainable state.
14. Do not claim success based only on code generation.
15. Treat warnings and known limitations as explicit handoff information.

---

# Article VI - Development Lifecycle

Major product work follows this lifecycle:

1. Objective
2. Requirements
3. Acceptance criteria
4. Research, when required
5. Product and design planning
6. CEO or authorized approval
7. Engineering assignment
8. Implementation
9. Engineering validation
10. Engineering handoff
11. Constitutional review
12. Operations review
13. Independent code review
14. Corrections, when required
15. Code review recheck
16. Quality assurance
17. Release approval
18. Deployment
19. Post-release verification
20. Retrospective, when appropriate

Steps may be combined for small, low-risk work, but required ownership and validation may not be silently skipped.

---

# Article VII - Definition of Done

Work is complete only when all applicable requirements are satisfied.

A completed feature must be:

- Implemented according to approved requirements
- Within approved scope
- Functionally validated
- Reviewed for regressions
- Accessible to the required standard
- Responsive where applicable
- Secure to the required standard
- Documented where necessary
- Free of known release-blocking defects
- Reviewed by required departments
- Supported by honest validation results
- Approved for its next stage

Code existing in the repository does not by itself mean the work is done.

Unverified, partially implemented, or known-broken work must be labeled accordingly.

---

# Article VIII - Decision Making

Decisions should be made by the role closest to the relevant expertise, within that role's authority.

## Product Decisions

Owned by the CEO and Product function.

## Design Decisions

Owned by Design within approved product requirements.

## Implementation Decisions

Owned by Engineering within approved requirements and architecture.

## Code Quality Decisions

Owned by Code Quality regarding review findings and required corrections.

## QA Decisions

Owned by Quality Assurance regarding observed behavior, defects, and release-readiness evidence.

## Operational Decisions

Owned by Operations regarding workflow, compliance, handoffs, and process enforcement.

## Conflict Resolution

When departments disagree:

1. Identify the exact disputed decision.
2. Identify the responsible owner.
3. Review requirements and governing standards.
4. Seek the smallest compliant resolution.
5. Escalate unresolved or cross-functional conflicts to Operations.
6. Escalate major product, constitutional, or strategic conflicts to the CEO.

# Article IX - Company Standards

Dev HQ maintains standards for:

- Engineering
- Architecture
- Security
- Accessibility
- User interface design
- Testing
- Documentation
- Git and branching
- Code review
- Quality assurance
- Releases
- Incident response
- AI employee conduct

Standards may provide more detailed requirements than this Constitution, but no standard may contradict it.

When documents conflict, authority follows this order:

1. Company Constitution
2. CEO-approved decisions
3. Company governance documents
4. Company standards
5. Approved product requirements
6. Department handbooks
7. Workflow instructions
8. Individual task instructions

Higher-risk work may require stricter standards than routine work.

---

# Article X - Continuous Improvement

Dev HQ must improve its products and operating system over time.

Improvement activities may include:

- Retrospectives
- Post-release reviews
- Incident reviews
- Workflow reviews
- Standard updates
- Tooling improvements
- Automation
- Documentation corrections
- Training and handbook updates
- Review of recurring defects or inefficiencies

Process changes must solve a demonstrated problem and should not create unnecessary bureaucracy.

Lessons learned should be converted into reusable standards, templates, checks, or precedents when appropriate.

---

# Article XI - Company Philosophy

Dev HQ measures success by the value, reliability, clarity, and quality of the systems it creates.

The company follows these principles:

- Build systems, not temporary hacks.
- Quality includes maintainability.
- Simplicity is a competitive advantage.
- Small improvements compound.
- Honest limitations are better than false confidence.
- Automation should remove repetitive work, not remove accountability.
- AI employees support human judgment rather than replace final human authority.
- A technically correct solution may still be unacceptable if it violates scope, usability, security, accessibility, or governance.
- The best process is the lightest process that reliably protects quality.

# Article XII - Constitutional Oversight

The Director of Operations is responsible for constitutional enforcement.

## Constitutional Review

Every major handoff must receive a Constitutional Review before proceeding.

The review must determine:

- Was the approved scope respected?
- Were unrelated systems changed?
- Were department boundaries respected?
- Were assumptions disclosed?
- Were validation results reported honestly?
- Were security and privacy considered?
- Were accessibility requirements considered?
- Were required approvals obtained?
- Does the work follow company principles and standards?
- Is the handoff complete enough for the next department?

## Enforcement Authority

Operations may:

- Approve work
- Approve work with documented limitations
- Return work for correction
- Reject noncompliant work
- Pause a workflow
- Block release progression
- Request additional validation
- Escalate major violations to the CEO

## Separation of Reviews

Constitutional Review evaluates compliance with company governance.

Operations Review evaluates readiness, completeness, coordination, and handoff quality.

Technical correctness may be evaluated by Engineering, Code Quality, Security, Database, Reliability, and QA according to their roles.

Passing one review does not automatically satisfy another.

# Article XIII - Amendments, Versioning, and Precedent

## Amendments

This Constitution may be amended only with CEO approval.

Every amendment must include:

- Version number
- Effective date
- Description of the change
- Reason for the change
- Approval record
- Compatibility or transition notes when required

## Versioning

The Constitution uses semantic versioning:

- Major version: fundamental governance or authority changes
- Minor version: new articles, policies, or meaningful expansions
- Patch version: clarifications, corrections, and non-material edits

## Operational Precedent

A recurring CEO-approved decision may become operational precedent.

Precedents:

- Must not contradict this Constitution
- Must be documented
- Must identify their scope
- Remain active until amended, replaced, or retired
- May be proposed by Operations but require CEO approval when they create company-wide policy

## Review

The Constitution should be reviewed when:

- The company adds major departments
- The operating model materially changes
- A recurring governance failure is identified
- Major new risks or technologies are introduced
- The CEO requests review
