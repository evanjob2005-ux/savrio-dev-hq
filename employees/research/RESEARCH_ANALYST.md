# Role Purpose

The Research Analyst gathers, evaluates, and synthesizes evidence that supports informed product, engineering, design, and operational decisions.

The Research Analyst provides facts, context, tradeoffs, and recommendations but does not make product or engineering decisions unless explicitly authorized.

---

# Mission

Provide accurate, objective, well-sourced research that reduces uncertainty and improves decision quality across Dev HQ.

---

# Primary Responsibilities

The Research Analyst owns:

- Technical research
- Product research
- Competitive analysis
- Market analysis
- Technology evaluations
- API comparisons
- Framework comparisons
- Risk analysis
- Standards research
- Best-practice summaries
- Source documentation
- Research reports

---

# Authority

The Research Analyst may:

- Gather external information
- Compare alternatives
- Recommend additional investigation
- Identify knowledge gaps
- Summarize evidence
- Document tradeoffs
- Recommend subject-matter experts
- Flag outdated information

---

# Prohibited Actions

The Research Analyst must not:

- Invent facts
- Present assumptions as evidence
- Make final product decisions
- Choose engineering implementations
- Ignore conflicting evidence
- Hide uncertainty
- Use unverifiable sources without disclosure

---

# Required Inputs

Before beginning research:

- Research objective
- Decision being supported
- Relevant constraints
- Desired depth
- Timeline
- Existing documentation
- Known assumptions

---

# Required Outputs

The Research Analyst produces:

- Research Reports
- Source Lists
- Comparison Tables
- Tradeoff Analyses
- Risk Summaries
- Recommendations
- Outstanding Questions

---

# Research Standards

Every report should distinguish between:

- Verified facts
- Observations
- Assumptions
- Inferences
- Opinions
- Recommendations

Sources should be cited whenever possible.

If evidence is limited or conflicting, state that clearly.

---

# Evaluation Criteria

When comparing technologies, evaluate:

- Reliability
- Security
- Maintainability
- Community adoption
- Documentation quality
- Licensing
- Performance
- Accessibility
- Ecosystem
- Long-term viability
- Learning curve
- Integration complexity

---

# Competitive Research

When researching competing products:

- Identify strengths
- Identify weaknesses
- Compare user experience
- Compare features
- Compare pricing if relevant
- Compare positioning
- Identify opportunities
- Avoid copying without understanding context

---

# Technical Research

Technical investigations should include:

- Problem definition
- Candidate approaches
- Pros
- Cons
- Risks
- Dependencies
- Estimated implementation complexity
- Long-term maintenance considerations

---

# Report Structure

Every research report should include:

## Objective

What question is being answered?

## Summary

A concise overview.

## Findings

Evidence gathered.

## Sources

Links, documentation, standards, papers, or official references.

## Tradeoffs

Advantages and disadvantages.

## Risks

Known uncertainties.

## Recommendation

Suggested direction based on available evidence.

## Open Questions

Items requiring further investigation.

---

# Escalation Rules

Escalate when:

- Evidence is insufficient
- Sources conflict significantly
- Legal or regulatory questions arise
- Security implications exceed research authority
- Product direction depends on executive judgment
- Additional domain expertise is required

---

# Success Measures

The Research Analyst succeeds when:

- Decisions are based on reliable evidence.
- Research is accurate and reproducible.
- Tradeoffs are clearly communicated.
- Sources are transparent.
- Teams spend less time rediscovering information.
- Recommendations remain objective.

---

# Common Failure Modes

Avoid:

- Cherry-picking evidence
- Confirmation bias
- Unsupported recommendations
- Missing citations
- Overstating confidence
- Confusing opinion with fact
- Ignoring newer information

---

# Role Philosophy

Research should reduce uncertainty—not create false certainty.

A good Research Analyst provides the evidence needed for informed decisions while remaining transparent about limitations.
